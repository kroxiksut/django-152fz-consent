# Legal text inventory and review map

This page is a single map of every legally significant text shipped with the
two packages: where it lives, what kind of text it is, and how it reaches a
running site. It is meant for a **legal reviewer** (or the operator's legal
counsel) who needs to read and adapt the texts, and for an integrator who needs
to know where those texts are managed.

A Russian version of this page is available at [legal-texts.ru.md](./legal-texts.ru.md).

## Read this first

The texts shipped with the packages are **starting samples**, not finished
legal documents. They are built with reference to 152-FZ and to the practice of
comparable solutions as current at the time of each release, but compliance
depends on how the operator configures and uses them. The package does not, by
itself, guarantee legal compliance — the legal correctness of documents, texts,
and processing workflows is the operator's responsibility, directly or through
legal counsel. See the "Commercial support" and "Legal notice" sections of the
[main README](../README.md).

Every shipped document revision is marked `legal_review_required=True` for
exactly this reason.

## Who should prepare these texts

Consent and policy texts should be prepared by the company's legal department or
external lawyers — not by administrators or developers. Texts copied from the
internet, or generated by AI, can be wrong or inapplicable to your processing
model; treat them as drafts at best. Administrators and developers wire the
texts into the admin and configure the workflow; the legal wording itself is a
legal task.

## Which texts you actually need

Prepare texts only for the consent flows and channels you actually use. In
particular, if you do not use the paper/offline (verified) consent variant —
i.e. you collect consent only on the web — you do not need to prepare the texts
specific to the paper flow. The optional verified-consent flow and its
paper-specific texts become relevant only once you enable it (see
[consent/verified-flow.md](./consent/en/verified-flow.md)).

## Where legal texts are managed: the admin

In a running project, all legal texts are managed in the **Django admin**, not
in source code:

- consent documents, their revisions, and processing purposes — in the consent
  admin;
- cookie banner text and policy revisions — in the cookies admin.

This is where you **add, edit, and delete** the texts that are actually served
to subjects, and therefore where legal review and authoring happen. You do not
need to touch the Python source to author production texts — review the admin
content.

The recommended flow keeps the shipped samples intact:

1. Let the package load the starter samples on first migrate.
2. Find the boxed sample revision in the admin.
3. Create a custom copy from it.
4. Edit the custom copy — this is where legal review happens.
5. Publish the custom revision as active.

Full detail lives in the module docs:

- Consent texts and document revisions — [consent/authoring.md](./consent/en/authoring.md)
- Cookie banner texts and presets — [cookies/presentation.md](./cookies/en/presentation.md)
- Settings and policy contract — [consent/configuration.md](./consent/en/configuration.md),
  [cookies/configuration.md](./cookies/en/configuration.md)

## Where the shipped samples come from (source)

The packages ship a set of **starting samples** that are bootstrapped into the
database on the first `post_migrate`. You normally leave these source files
**as-is** and do all real work in the admin. They are listed here only for
transparency and for the rare case where you want to change the defaults that
get bootstrapped — not as files a reviewer has to edit.

| Text | Source location | Kind |
|------|-----------------|------|
| Sample consent documents, processing policy, and personal-data notices (Russian) | `src/django_consent_152fz/box_templates/documents.py` | Boxed/sample document revisions |
| Starter processing purposes — titles and descriptions | `src/django_consent_152fz/core/starter_purposes.py` | Boxed/sample seed data |
| Cookie banner text presets (RU + EN variants) | `src/django_cookies_152fz/models.py` | Boxed/sample banner revision text |
| Consent UI labels, form fields, help texts | `src/django_consent_152fz/locale/{ru,en}/LC_MESSAGES/django.po` | Interface strings (gettext) — not legal documents |
| Cookie UI labels, form fields, help texts | `src/django_cookies_152fz/locale/{ru,en}/LC_MESSAGES/django.po` | Interface strings (gettext) — not legal documents |
| Demo site copy and long-form demo texts | `demo/common/texts/{ru,en}/` | **Demo-only** content |

Notes on the table:

- **Sample documents, starter purposes, and banner presets** are exactly that —
  *samples*. The live texts an operator serves are the admin revisions created
  from them; review the admin versions, not these source files.
- **`.po` translation files contain interface labels only** (admin captions,
  field names, help text) — not the legal documents themselves. They matter for
  terminology consistency, but they are not the place to author consent or
  policy text.
- **`demo/common/texts/` is demo-only.** It illustrates a training-center
  scenario and is not part of either published package. Do not treat it as a
  production legal source.

## Review checklist for legal counsel

For each consent document and the processing policy, confirm that the text
states at least:

- who the operator is;
- the specific processing purpose it covers;
- the categories or data fields actually processed in this flow;
- how the subject confirms consent;
- how the subject can withdraw consent;
- where the linked document is, if the consent is part of a larger package.

Watch for the common problems the sample texts are explicitly not protected
against:

- one generic text reused across all forms without checking its meaning;
- listing data that is not actually collected in a given flow;
- copying text from another project without adapting it to the real processing
  model.

For the cookie banner, confirm that category descriptions, the policy
reference, and the re-consent wording match how cookies are actually used on
the site.

## Replacing texts for other jurisdictions

The Russian texts and cookie policy templates can be replaced with texts for
other jurisdictions. The mechanics (authoring, configuration, presets) are the
same — only the content changes. Start from the authoring and configuration
docs linked above, and apply the same review checklist to the replacement
texts.
